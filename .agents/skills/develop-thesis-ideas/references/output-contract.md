# Output Contract

Use the smallest structure that answers the request. For a substantial ideation, validation, comparison, or synthesis artifact, include the following sections.

## 1. Inquiry

- Question being answered.
- Mode: Explore, Validate, Synthesize, Compare, or Decide.
- Scope and assumptions.

## 2. Bottom line

Lead with the current conclusion and confidence. State whether it is verified, inferred, hypothetical, or unresolved.

## 3. Evidence map

Use a compact table when several claims or works must be compared:

| Claim or issue | Evidence | Status | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|

Include supporting and contradicting evidence. Link to primary sources.

## 4. Candidate cards

For each serious candidate:

- research question;
- falsifiable hypothesis;
- closest prior work;
- residual gap;
- minimal method and data;
- primary metric;
- main failure mode;
- useful negative result;
- one-year minimum scope.

Omit this section when the task is only validating one claim.

## 5. Red-team findings

List the strongest reasons the idea may be non-novel, infeasible, invalid, unsafe, or misaligned with its claimed deployment value. Address scope inflation explicitly.

## 6. Recommendation

Recommend only when requested. Explain trade-offs and confidence rather than presenting a score as fact.

## 7. Validation contract

- Next smallest test or search.
- Success signal.
- Kill or narrow condition.
- Pivot if the hypothesis fails.
- Remaining unknowns and search limitations.

## 8. Project-state impact

State whether `PROJECT.md`, `TASKS.md`, or `DECISIONS.md` should change. Treat `DECISIONS.md` as author-owned and read-only: provide an exact proposed entry when useful, but never edit it. Do not present an unapproved recommendation as a recorded decision.
