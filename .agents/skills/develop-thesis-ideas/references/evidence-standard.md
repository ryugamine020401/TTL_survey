# Evidence Standard

## Evidence states

Use these labels when the distinction matters:

- **Verified:** Directly supported by an inspected primary or authoritative source.
- **Inference:** A reasoned conclusion drawn from cited evidence; state the inference.
- **Hypothesis:** A falsifiable proposition proposed for research.
- **Unknown:** Material information not established by the inspected evidence.

## Source priority

For technical claims, prefer:

1. Published paper or official proceedings page.
2. Official preprint from the authors when no published version exists.
3. Official dataset, specification, or author-maintained repository.
4. High-quality secondary survey for discovery and context.

Do not use search-result snippets, vendor marketing, role-play discussion, or model memory as final support for a technical claim.

## Novelty checks

A novelty check must record:

- search date;
- databases or search systems used;
- core query families and synonyms;
- date range and domain boundaries;
- closest competing works;
- what those works already contribute;
- the narrower residual gap, if any;
- limitations that could still overturn the conclusion.

Avoid absolute novelty language. Prefer bounded wording such as:

> Within the documented search scope, no inspected work jointly evaluates X under Y using Z; the closest works cover A and B separately.

## Contradictions

When sources conflict:

1. Check whether they use different datasets, shifts, metrics, operating points, or threat models.
2. Check publication status and chronology.
3. Preserve both results when the conflict is not resolved.
4. Turn a meaningful unresolved conflict into a candidate research question.

## Gap types

Name the gap precisely:

- method gap;
- evaluation or metric gap;
- dataset, language, channel, or population gap;
- threat-model or adaptive-attack gap;
- causal or measurement gap;
- deployment or usability gap;
- replication or independent-audit gap.

A new combination of familiar components is not automatically a contribution. Explain why the interaction creates a non-obvious question or new evidence.
